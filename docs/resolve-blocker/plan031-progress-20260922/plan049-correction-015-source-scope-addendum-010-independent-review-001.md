2. **The durable operation key does not establish an exact owner across processes.** Section 2 forms keys using the root token and an “exact handle object ID,” and retains a mutex in an in-process `stop_group` registry. A language-runtime object ID is scoped to one process lifetime and may be copied by fork or reused; a mutex and retained receipt in that process are not an interprocess owner identity. Thus the journal cannot tell whether a later process presenting the same numeric object ID is the process that executed the one root wait, pidfd send, or descriptor close. Nor does the text specify how a forked/copied registry and descriptor are invalidated, or how loss of the original owner is distinguished from a later same-handle caller. Specify a durable, unique owner/process-incarnation and handle-registration token, bound under the ledger lock to the retained object and descriptor and checked on every receipt replay; define fork/restart and registry-loss behavior. Only that exact live owner may complete a cached wait/signal/close receipt. Other claimants must consume an already durable result or fail closed without another syscall. The in-process registry must atomically supply one mutex to all local callers. Without these rules, the promised exclusive per-handle and interprocess ownership is not proved.

3. **The repeated same-handle wait rule contradicts adopted addendum006.** Addendum006 explicitly requires its already-retired exact-handle `stop_group` path to preserve the existing final census/return behavior **including `process.wait` on that same handle**. Its independent PASS repeats that requirement. Addendum010 sections 2 and 4 instead permit only one OS root wait and say the already-retired path emits “no ... repeated wait/close.” A cached `Popen.wait()` invocation and a second OS wait might be different operations, but the proposal does not make that distinction or say which earlier requirement it supersedes. Resolve the exact method-call and OS-wait behavior of the known direct-call-plus-registered-cleanup case, preserve its deadline, return, census, and cleanup-error precedence, and explicitly identify any narrow change to addendum006 for authorization and source review. A source implementer cannot satisfy both literal rules as written.

The readback-first predicate is otherwise a sound fail-closed model for append/readback failure: reread the complete journal under the interprocess lock, consume one exact matching event, append only when absent, and reject conflicting or duplicate events. A successful pidfd send with an unavailable durable result must never be sent again; an intent without a proven result and without the exact live owner's receipt blocks later signaling and retirement. The same rule applies to root-wait and pin-close receipts. Root wait must be claimed before the single blocking OS wait and may remain charged permanently on owner loss. Descendant retirement follows terminal and durable pin-close evidence; root retirement follows exact wait, descendants, reservations, and final census. These are plan requirements, not verified source behavior. The original absolute cleanup deadline, immediate pre-signal/pre-wait checks, TERM grace, cleanup-error precedence, legacy no-ledger behavior, original assertions, 78/249/1,028 counts, CPU-only serial scope, `B+max(1,H)≤8`, 150 GiB artifact cap, and 64 MiB memo cap remain required.
