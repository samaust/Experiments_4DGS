## Required descendant acknowledgment and stop order

Every tracked process descendant must have a durable creator-ancestry acknowledgment while the relationship is still live and observable. Immediately after the existing successful reservation/bind of a process launched by a B root, the ledger records and fsyncs an acknowledgment binding its exact descendant token to the owning root token, creator process/thread identity, child boot ID/PID/start ticks/PGID/PPID, and the creation-time census. Read back and validate that record before returning the process handle to its caller. Any uncertain creation, identity, ancestry, append, fsync, or readback remains charged and must not be returned as a successful registered child. This creator-time record allows later cleanup to identify a child after its PPID changes. The L35 release fixture still performs its separate existing live-census acknowledgment after observing the PID marker and before release; the creator-time acknowledgment does not replace or weaken that race-specific step.

Within the existing `stop_group(process, deadline)` call, use one ordered owner for the complete stop and retirement transition:

1. Resolve the exact live B root token from the retained process handle; enumerate all non-retired descendant ledger tokens under that root. For each token require its durable creator acknowledgment, exact stable identity, and a fresh live `/proc` census matching boot ID, PID, start ticks and the recorded PGID. Do not require the original PPID to remain current after reparenting. Open a pidfd for each exact live descendant and immediately re-read/reconcile its identity before any cleanup signal. If a tracked descendant cannot be found or pinned, retain the entire tree charge and fail cleanup closed.
2. Preserve the existing TERM, grace wait, KILL, group-empty wait, exact `process.wait`, and original deadline behavior. For a pinned descendant that is no longer in the target PGID, do not silently assume group signals covered it; keep the job unresolved and fail closed. The proposal does not authorize a new timeout or unbounded wait.
3. Before the existing `retire_owned(process.pid)` call, require the original group-empty result and successful wait on that exact worker handle, then require each pinned pidfd to report terminal. Append the identity-bound descendant-retired events with durable readback. Only after every tracked descendant is retired call the existing `retire_owned(process.pid)` once; it emits the matching root wait/retirement transition. Do not call it a second time and do not append duplicate root retirement. Keep pidfds open through their terminal/readback checks, then close them on success. Any timeout, signal/wait ambiguity, pidfd uncertainty, ledger error, or readback failure leaves the affected job/tree charged and preserves existing primary/secondary cleanup behavior.
4. Preserve `stop_group`'s existing return value and final group census. With no `S1_JOB_LEDGER`, existing callers retain current behavior without new ledger requirements. Exact-source review must inspect every existing caller, not only the two integration cases, for both ledger-enabled and legacy paths.

The two named existing tests must assert the new descendant and root-tree terminal records while retaining every original assertion in place. Existing L35 assertions and marker→live-census→ledger-ack→pidfd-before-release behavior also remain unchanged. Unregistered processes are outside this logical-ledger claim; no kernel-wide or cgroup containment claim is added.
