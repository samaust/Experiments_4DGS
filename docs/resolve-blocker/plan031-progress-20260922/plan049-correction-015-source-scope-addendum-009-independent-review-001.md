## Blocking findings

1. **The required transitive proof cannot currently pass its own nine-path rule.** Section 3 requires a map of *all* code executed by selected child processes, including imported project entrypoints that can create processes, but then limits that mapping to code within the nine authorized source paths. Existing selected commands execute `vipe_benchmark.s1_cpu_helper` from `s1_helper_session.py:1012-1014` and `vipe_benchmark.s1_validation_runner` from `test_vipe_benchmark_supervisor.py:856`; neither module is one of the nine paths. The fixture also imports `s1_cpu_helper` in its child process at `test_vipe_benchmark_s1_helper_fixtures.py:72`. The proposal does not provide a reviewed proof that those modules and their reachable child code cannot create an unmanaged process, change group/session, or execute another helper. Its instruction to stop for an amendment if proof is unavailable is sound, and that condition is already met on the presented scope. Amend the audit boundary explicitly to permit a **read-only, hash-bound transitive audit** of these and all other reachable project modules, while preserving the nine-path *edit* limit; or name the precise additional source paths and seek the separate reviewed scope/containment amendment before a PASS. The audit must identify nested command bytes and exact creator/group-change call sites, then stop on any unmediated path. A group census cannot fill this proof gap.

2. **A durable signal intent does not establish one sender.** Section 4 has `signal-intent` before the syscall and `signal-sent` afterward, but it does not assign an exclusive owner or hold a lock through the intent/send/readback interval. Two cleanup callers can both see a target without `signal-sent`, each append an intent, and each send TERM to the same pinned target. The phrase “an unsent target can proceed only after fresh locked ledger verification” is unsafe if a durable intent without `signal-sent` is treated as unsent: the first syscall may already have succeeded and its owner may have failed or crashed before the success append. Define one durable per-target/per-signal claim, the exact in-process and interprocess exclusion/owner rule through the syscall and readback, and the permitted same-owner continuation. An intent without durable success must become unknown on owner loss or any ambiguous outcome, with no resend or later-target send. A second caller must not create another intent or infer that a send has not happened. Preserve the original absolute deadline check immediately before the one permitted syscall and the original TERM-to-KILL conditions.

3. **Volatile wait and close receipts need an exact duplicate-safe replay rule.** Section 2 refers to an “existing per-handle in-process single-flight lock,” but does not identify its lifecycle or how every same-handle cleanup caller acquires it; the reviewed `supervisor.stop_group` has no such lock. More importantly, an append can succeed while its readback fails. “Retry only that receipt's durable append” could append a second `root-wait`, and the identical gap exists for `pin-closed` after a successful close. Define the lock/owner lifecycle, a receipt event key and replay predicate, and a readback-first retry that consumes the already-durable matching event or appends once with specified duplicate-safe semantics. A different or lost owner must remain charged without another wait or close-by-number. Apply the same rule when the root exits during pin acquisition. Do not declare `waited` or root `retired` until the matching wait, terminal, and close evidence is durably replayable.
