2. **Correction005 protocol fixes: preserved and clarified.** The separate S2/C2 acknowledgments, generated-start exception to original-payload equality, no nonce, I2/F2 intent binding, acyclic proof/admission sequence and two-stage path gate carry forward. Ordinary partial output chunks may accumulate; incomplete EOF, truncation, duplicate output or unresolved exchanges cannot advance the state. That resolves the prior review's fragmentation wording concern. Exact parser/nested identity/archive schemas and real transport capability remain unproved. The new cgroup evidence will also need fixed path families and binding rules: any preparation evidence created before driver entry must be placed and classified without silently violating the existing two-bootstrap-file allowlist.

3. **Observed broad scope versus candidate child: correctly separated.** The document labels the terminal scope with reported `pids.current=131` and `pids.max=143286` as broad, non-enforcing for this attempt, and explicitly denies that visibility proves delegation or tool-session membership. It proposes a new exclusively named per-attempt child rather than changing that scope's limits. This is appropriate. I did not reread host cgroup files or independently confirm those reported observations; no current capacity or authority follows from them. The candidate child does not yet exist by virtue of this proposal.

4. **`pids.max=8` does not itself enforce the Plan049 formula.** The document notices the key arithmetic correctly: when H is positive, the charge is B+H; when H is zero, it is B+1. Thus a total-task cap8 permits B=8,H=0, while the contract requires B≤7. A subsequent census rejecting B=8 would detect a violation, not prevent it. “At every creation” and reserving a simultaneous peak must be implemented as a complete, serialized creation/transition gate covering all reachable task/thread creation and any change in B/H classification. Otherwise transient or concurrent creation may evade samples while remaining below the kernel's cap. The proposal has not supplied that mechanism for tool transport or native threads. It is therefore a conditional combination of backstop plus prospective software checks, not continuous enforcement of the unchanged formula. A later design could establish complete gates, enforce an appropriate driver-subtree cap with exact phase/classification semantics, or propose a conservative task cap that implies the formula; no such alternative is adopted by this review. A sampled count plus cap8 alone cannot clear C3.

5. **Kernel control semantics require explicit topology and migration rules.** Kernel documentation states that the pids controller counts tasks/TIDs and constrains fork/clone; attaching tasks can exceed `pids.max`. `pids.current` includes descendants, while task-list reads are not an atomic identity snapshot. Controller availability differs from enabling a controller for children through `cgroup.subtree_control`. Delegation and migration have separate permissions, and threaded versus domain topology changes membership interfaces. Zombie processes can be absent from `cgroup.procs`, so emptiness alone is not proof of reaping. [Linux kernel cgroup v2 documentation](https://docs.kernel.org/admin-guide/cgroup-v2.html)

   Consequently, this design must specify the exact dedicated attempt-root path, cgroup type, applicable mount/event semantics, already-enabled controllers, who can change limits/membership, and how unintended ingress/egress is prevented or rejected. “Parent pids.current” must mean the dedicated attempt root that recursively covers writer/driver subgroups, not its broad host parent. Identity/census correlation needs a stable procedure across namespace views and all leaves, not equality of independently sampled scalar counts. The plan's before-first-task placement requirement appropriately excludes late migration as startup evidence, but it does not itself specify subsequent containment or permission management. No new controller/membership write is authorized by implication.

6. **Opaque tool-session placement and observation remain the primary capability blocker.** The critical coverage gate correctly acknowledges that the exposed tool has not supplied atomic session placement, handle-to-host-root mapping or full wrapper/PTY coverage. A user-created child and a command that moves itself there cannot establish the membership of wrappers created earlier outside it. A probe of an unrelated subprocess launcher cannot validate the actual exec/write_stdin tool path. The proposed mechanism must identify a real callable launch-service facility and its guarantees before running dependent commands. It must classify every attempt-owned task, including probe supervisor/observer, shell and transport, and account for their simultaneous peaks; a new monitoring process outside the group cannot be ignored. Mere samples during a successful probe cannot prove all future first-task containment without reviewed launcher/kernel semantics and control of relevant migration/creation paths. The document correctly keeps failure to demonstrate coverage as a stop, rather than allowing a fixed H or self-report fallback.

7. **Write/probe scope is gated, but the exact probe is missing.** “Order before any diagnostic” requires independent review of an exact synthetic probe before Main runs it. Correction006 supplies acceptance goals, not that exact probe: there is no approved delegated path, exact operation list, startup mechanism, task budget per phase, success/failure/negative-control procedure, or cleanup procedure for partially successful creation. Its allowed child-directory/`pids.max` writes may be insufficient if the chosen hierarchy requires `cgroup.subtree_control`, membership or service-manager operations; those must be named and reviewed, or an already-configured facility must be demonstrated through read-only evidence. The design properly forbids parent-limit changes and unrelated migrations, and preserves the repository's single safe escalated retry/stop policy for later authorized operations. This review is not that operation approval and performs no retry. A hard-cap rejection probe must itself stay within Plan049 charge, including observers and setup/cleanup tasks; “synthetic” is not an exemption. “Bounded” should mean a fixed scope/finite operation design here, not a newly invented operational wall-clock ceiling.

8. **Retirement/cleanup and evidence binding need a complete finite procedure.** Requiring actual tool terminals, full identity reconciliation, empty descendant subgroups and no live task before removal is stronger than a scalar empty count and is directionally correct. The exact wait/reap evidence and cleanup owner must be specified, including failure after child creation or after setting a limit. No `cgroup.kill`, forced migration or system-manager reset is authorized by the stated removal rule. Any process used to preserve final cgroup evidence or remove the group needs explicit accounting and placement; it cannot be both silently outside the accounting boundary and relied upon as a complete observer. The accepted transcript boundary now allows a finite truthful record of that final cleanup instead of another self-archiving chain. Group lifecycle records must bind kind/index, exact host path/identity, policy, source, handles and observations to the future authority/proof policy without adding a hash cycle or reusing a retired group/index.
